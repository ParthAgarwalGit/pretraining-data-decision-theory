# P1-06 finding: is `sigma2_extrap` large, small, or task-dependent?

**Short answer: task-dependent, and large enough to explain the observed
"extrapolation does not win" result — but the bound built on top of it is
too loose to make that a precise quantitative prediction.**

## The decomposition itself

*(Regenerated 2026-09-20 on the repaired fitters and the calibrated
squared-bias estimator; the numbers below replace the earlier run's.)*

Across all 6 fitters × 3 designs × 11 tasks × 25 recipes (0 of 1,980,000
bootstrap fits failed), `sigma2_extrap_hat` is clearly **task-dependent**:
for `PowerLawN` at the `<=150M` design, the per-task median across recipes
ranges from 0.0019 (`piqa`) to 0.0546 (`hellaswag`) — a ~28x spread on the same
fitter and design, just different tasks (`LogLinear`: ~31x). For every fitter
except `TwoStepLadder`, the bias-squared estimate is 200–1,600x the
estimation variance `v_hat` in the median cell (`docs/decisions.md`,
2026-09-20). `TwoStepLadder` is the exception: its `v_hat` is large (median
~6e-3) and its bias is not distinguishable from zero — 6 of 11 tasks have a
median clipped estimate of exactly 0. Overall 13.6% of cells (672/4950) have a
negative *unclipped* estimate (bias undetectable against the variance);
the clipped field reports 0 for them and is a heuristic, biased upward for small
true bias, so averages should use the unclipped field
(`results/p1_06_decomposition.json`).

## The surprising part: the ratio moves the wrong way

The plan's own stated signature prediction — `sigma2_extrap_hat / v_hat`
should *grow* with fitting compute, because `v` shrinks and `sigma2_extrap`
does not — **does not hold**. For every one of the 6 fitters, the ratio
*falls* as the design grows from `<=150M` to `<=530M` (median per-cell ratio,
e.g. PowerLawN: 359.7 → 305.7 → 274.1; ChinchillaND: 376.5 → 306.7 → 274.1;
ConstantExtrapolator: 1611 → 573 → 194; TwoStepLadder: 0.15 → 0.00 → 0.00).
The earlier run's small PowerLawN/PowerLawC ratios (5–32) were artifacts of
power-law fits stuck at flat exponents before the initialization repair; on
the repaired fitters they behave like ChinchillaND and LogLinear. Both
quantities shrink as the design grows, but `sigma2_extrap_hat` shrinks faster
than `v_hat`. Plausible explanation: a
design whose largest size is closer to the 1B target is extrapolating a
shorter relative distance, which should reduce bias more than it reduces
an already-well-identified fit's bootstrap variance (`v_hat` is close to
flat across designs for most fitters, suggesting these fits aren't
variance-starved even at the smallest design). See `docs/decisions.md`
(2026-09-04, "P1-06 finding") for the full per-fitter table.

## Does the bound explain the ~80% ceiling? (P1-07/P1-08)

P1-07 found the bound is never violated (tightness ratio >= 1 in all
396 cells) and that the pairwise-difference form is tighter than the
marginal form (median ratio 20.6 vs. 24.1). **That is not confirmation of
the theory:** every one of the 396 bounds is at least 1, so any empirical
error probability satisfies it -- the check cannot tell a valid bound from
an invalid one (the original additive form is in fact invalid; PR #17), and
the tightness ratios compare two vacuous quantities. (External review of
PR #21.)

But P1-08 found that taken *literally* as a quantitative accuracy
predictor, the bound is **vacuous**: `bound_pairwise` exceeds 1 in all
396 cells (a union bound over ~24 mostly-near-tied comparisons per task —
traceable to P1-02's own finding that 9 of 11 tasks have no statistically
resolvable winner at all), so the clipped predicted accuracy is 0.0%
everywhere. The theory correctly never claims something impossible, but
at these gap sizes it also gives no informative number to compare
against the observed 60–85% range.

The `sigma2_extrap = 0` counterfactual: removing bias alone lets only 1 of
15 (fitter, design) pairs beat single-scale's own bias-free counterfactual
(`LogLinear` at `<=530M`, the deliberately misspecified — hence low-variance
— model). It runs on the same vacuous bound and compares extrapolation's
bound to single-scale's bound at the design's endpoint model only (an
unmatched-compute comparison), so it is inconclusive about bias rather than
a qualified confirmation.

## Bottom line for the paper framing

`sigma2_extrap` is real, task-dependent, and large enough that it plausibly
explains why extrapolation doesn't beat single-scale in this data — the
qualitative story (Claim 1/2) is consistent with it. The *quantitative* plug-in
bound built from it is vacuous (at least 1 in every cell, so "never violated"
carries no weight) and cannot predict the ~80% ceiling as a number,
mainly because most tasks' ground truth is itself ambiguous (P1-02). This
argues for the paper leading with the qualitative decomposition result
and the "9/11 tasks are ambiguous" finding as co-equal headline claims,
rather than presenting the bound's numeric tightness as the main
quantitative payoff.
